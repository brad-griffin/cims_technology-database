Iron and steel, as the hierarchy now stands

Nodes are the <sector>.iron-and-steel.* rows after the iron-form restructure (section 3 of the harmonization proposal). Arrows show material and energy moving from the node that supplies it to the node that uses it, so they run opposite to CIMS service requests.

Not drawn: shared services

These are in the branch and requested across the site. Wiring each one to every user would bury the process chain, so they're listed here instead.

  • service.fuel-blend.{methane, diesel}: blend commodities into supply for the fired nodes and mobile equipment.
  • service.machine-drive.{compressed-air, fans-and-blowers, materials-handling, materials-processing, pumps}. The seven-sector Direct Drive is folded into these as motor efficiency variants.
  • service.hvac.{space-heating, space-cooling} and service.lighting.
  • service.steam is requested across the site. The only supply drawn into it here is process gas.

How to read it

  • Iron and steel are separate nodes, joined by the form of iron: liquid hot metal goes to the BOF, and solid DRI or scrap goes to the EAF. This lets a blast furnace retire at a reline while the BOF shop keeps running.
  • Scrap availability is a market-share limit on the scrap-EAF technology, not a node.
  • The CCS links are inferred from technology names and the stream split: shaft-furnace reformer gas is concentrated, and blast-furnace, sinter and EAF off-gas is diffuse. Check them against the model's request rows before relying on them.
  • product.foundry stands alone. It melts scrap and purchased pig iron, including Sorel-Tracy's, and takes no hot metal from the integrated route.
