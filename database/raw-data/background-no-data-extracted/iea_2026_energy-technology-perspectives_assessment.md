# ETP 2026 — assessed, nothing extractable

`iea_2026_energy-technology-perspectives.pdf`, assessed 2026-08-29. **No technology
parameters were extracted, and none should be expected from this document.** Recorded
here so the search is not repeated.

## What it is

388 pages on the **deployment, manufacturing and trade** of clean energy technologies:
project pipelines, manufacturing capacity, trade flows, tariffs, supply-chain
concentration and industrial competitiveness. The 2026 edition puts a special focus on
supply-chain vulnerabilities and manufacturing cost structures.

Chapters: ten key questions · outlook for deployment · technology deployment dashboards ·
recent trends · outlook for manufacturing and trade · supply chain risks · industrial
competitiveness · strategic considerations. The annexes are definitions, glossary,
regional groupings and references — **there is no technology-assumptions annex**, which
is where a report of this kind would carry costs and efficiencies if it had them.

## What was checked

- **338 ruled "tables" across 122 pages.** Sampled: they are chart frames, layout boxes
  and policy tables. The largest real one is effective trade-weighted tariff rates by
  product and region — trade policy, not technology parameters.
- **Every table and figure caption** matching steel, cement, chemicals, ammonia,
  industry, hydrogen, heat or electrolysis. The industrially relevant items are all
  *figures*: hydrogen production cost by pathway and region (1.11), production costs of
  steel and urea (1.17), industrial user energy prices (7.4), downstream multipliers for
  iron and steel (7.7). **Figures are charts; their underlying data is not in the PDF.**

## What to do instead

- The **ETP Clean Energy Technology Guide** (`iea_2024_001`) is the companion dataset and
  *is* extractable — 640 technologies with a 2020–2024 TRL series, already processed.
- If the figure data is wanted, IEA publishes chart data separately on the report's web
  page; that would be a new download, not something recoverable from this file.

The source stays registered (`iea_2026_001`) and held: it is a legitimate citation for
deployment and supply-chain statements, just not for technology parameters.
